About Lou

Eugene Hynes, Lou to most people who know him, grew up in Twillingate, where he was already picking litter off the trails and beaches as a kid. Today he works full time, lives on the Avalon, and spends his days off in the water.

It started in the summer of 2017. Swimming with his stepchildren in Topsail Pond, he noticed bottles, cans, golf balls and plastic on the bottom, so he put on his snorkel gear and brought it all ashore. A couple of days later, at Rotary Sunshine Park in St. John’s, he found the same thing. That evening he started planning how to do it properly.

The method hasn’t changed much since. He paddles out, snorkels and free-dives along the shoreline, and fills his kayak with whatever is down there, often towing a second kayak as a barge. Tires, bottles, cans, fishing gear, and now and then something old and remarkable. Back on land he sorts it all: roughly 80 per cent can be recycled or reused, and the rest goes where it belongs. Aluminum pull tabs go to Ronald McDonald House. In spring and fall, when the water is too cold, he works the pond edges, the beaches and the T’Railway instead.

For a few seasons his co-worker Jason Brushett dove alongside him. Friends, family and followers of the page still pitch in, whether by offering their recyclables or covering the cost of a cleanup.

We do not inherit the earth from our ancestors, we borrow it from our children. One person can make a big difference!

Lou, on the Avalon Pond Cleanups page, July 2018

He photographs every haul and posts it to the Avalon Pond Cleanups Facebook page. This site gathers those posts onto one map, so you can find any pond he has done and see every cleanup there.

Awards and recognition

  1. 2026
    Recognized in the House of Assembly

    Lou’s ten years of pond cleanups were recognized in the Newfoundland and Labrador House of Assembly in November 2026, with praise from Mount Scio MHA Sarah Stoodley. Lou was there in the public gallery. That year alone he pulled 120 vehicle tires out of Paddys Pond.

  2. MMSB
    Champion for a Greener Future

    The Multi-Materials Stewardship Board lists Avalon Pond Cleanups among its Champions for a Greener Future, for sorting what he recovers so that most of it is recycled rather than landfilled.
